# Scope and attribution for the cutoff-six baseline and cutoff-seven revision

Checked 26 November 2026. Our upper size is n for the comparison of rows at n−1 and n. The parameter k always refers to a minimum allowed cycle length or to the corresponding beginning of the unit tail.

- Alexandersson's catalog defines the exact strict-excedance sum on permutations with every cycle of length at least k. Its conjecture asks for consecutive interlacing for all sizes from the first nonempty positive-size row onward. The catalog reports finite evidence through n=22 for k≤6. Its ordinary-descent question is a separate entry and uses a different statistic. [Original conjecture, recurrence and evidence](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#permutations-with-long-cycles)
- ProgramComputer's Theorems 1 and 2 cover the unweighted cutoff-three case at all sizes and an absolute quadratic eventual threshold for hard cutoffs, using lower size in the adjacent pair. The two-cycle theorem covers lower size j with k≤j≤3k−2, hence upper sizes 7 through 17 at k=6. The numerical-cutoff theorem gives all hard cutoffs simultaneously for lower size at least 10²⁰. Its displayed short-cycle condition (U1) in fact applies at k=6 for lower size at least 10⁶. At that point j/1200>30 log j+20, and the gap is increasing thereafter. This last range is a specialization of its stated sufficient condition. [Pinned Theorems 1 and 2](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4da48b72ed8f92f96c2faf60e2d63d253b3b507a/topics/long-cycle-excedances/paper.tex) · [Pinned two-cycle theorem](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4da48b72ed8f92f96c2faf60e2d63d253b3b507a/topics/long-cycle-excedances/two-cycle-insertion.tex) · [Pinned uniform theorem and U1](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4da48b72ed8f92f96c2faf60e2d63d253b3b507a/topics/long-cycle-excedances/effective-interlacing.tex)
- Brenti's Theorem 3.1 gives the product for a prescribed cycle type and Theorem 3.2 gives real-rootedness for each such class. Theorems 3.3–3.4 prove symmetric unimodality for sums over cycle-length restrictions, including r-derangements. Section 5.5 asks about real-rootedness. Individual cycle classes and symmetric unimodality do not provide consecutive root order for these sums. [Brenti, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 157 (1993)](https://msp.org/pjm/1993/157-1/pjm-v157-n1-p01-s.pdf)
- Ma's Theorem 3.5 proves a Sturm property for derangement excedance rows with a common factor q>0 for each cycle. The weight is q raised to the number of all cycles. Independently varying four length-specific weights with fixed unit weights on long cycles gives another family. The ordinary derangement anchor occurs at q=1. [Ma, Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society 85 (2012)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/B71BC6B211BDB0AE2C0D61B5176CA294/S0004972711002760a.pdf/counting_permutations_by_numbers_of_excedances_fixed_points_and_cycles.pdf)
- Rakotoson and Rakotondrajao's Theorem 30 gives the finer associated Stirling–Eulerian enumeration from which the hard-cutoff recurrence follows by summing. It tracks the number of cycles as well as the excedance statistic. [Journal of Integer Sequences, Article 25.5.7](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL28/Rakotoson/rakoto12.html)
- The author's preceding cutoff-five release gives the hard cutoff-five result and the three-mark cube starting at upper size 49. It is the specialization w₅=1 of the new analytic family in that range. The current candidate keeps the frozen, earlier quadratic statement and includes the complete hard-six range and the four-mark result from upper size 49. [Public cutoff-five release](https://github.com/AlexZander-666/long-cycle-excedance-interlacing/releases/tag/v1.1.0)

The targeted searches used “minimum cycle length six excedance real-rooted interlacing polynomial”, “cutoff six excedance interlacing permutations weights all sizes”, “permutations cycle length at least six excedances zeros interlace”, and repository-targeted variants with “Alexandersson”, “ProgramComputer”, and “k=6”. They located the sources above, plus work on fixed-point and uniform cycle marks. The sources inspected did not give an all-size cutoff-six theorem or the four-coordinate closed cube. This is a bounded search. The manuscript gives exact comparisons and makes no exhaustive priority claim.
